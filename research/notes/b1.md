## Concept definitions (merged)

### Swing high / swing low / legs
- Says: highs and lows are the turning points of "waves"; "draw your structure as waves, averaging everything out"; use a line chart to simplify (fg ~[68:48]-[71:50]). A "leg of structure is defined from the low to the high" (fg [46:32]).
- Objective proposal: fractal pivot with lookback N bars each side (parameter N per TF, e.g. 3-5 for execution TF, larger for HTF), or ZigZag with minimum reversal threshold = k*ATR(14) (k 1.5-3). Swing leg = last confirmed opposite pivots.
- Ambiguity: he never gives a size threshold; "notable pull away ... standard zigzag pattern" (fg [54:38]) is eyeballing. He explicitly refuses to count a sloppy continuation as a correction ("I would just consider this the full swing move"). Pivot is only confirmed N bars later (see red flags).

### Market structure, BOS, CHoCH-equivalent
- Says: trend up = HH+HL, down = LL+LH, else consolidation; BOS = "a point where structure has broken through a previous high or a previous low" (fg [38:28]); he does NOT use "CHoCH"; he just calls every break BOS ("I still just call them breaks of structure", fg [60:42]). Reversal = "impulse followed by impulse in opposite direction"; "all it takes to shift the trend ... candles go and close above the previous high" (fg [59:40]). Corrective low is "validated" only retroactively when the next BOS prints (fg [45:31]-[46:32]).
- Proposed objective: maintain last confirmed swing high SH and swing low SL. BOS_up when a bar CLOSES > SH (body close, not wick). Trend state = bull after BOS_up; the last confirmed swing low before the BOS becomes the "structural low"; bear flips when close < structural low. Reversal flag = BOS in the opposite direction of current trend (this is a CHoCH by other people's naming). Consolidation = neither (e.g. both HH and LL inside last X swings, or range contraction), no trade.
- Ambiguity: (1) which swing counts as "structural" high/low vs noise - discretionary wave-averaging; (2) a close above merely "an internal high" is allowed to be BOS internally but not externally, depends on TF he picks; (3) "consolidation" has no objective criterion (he says HH+LL = consolidation, fg [40:28], but then also treats HH/LL expansions in trend as tradeable); (4) in Jh [11:08]-[12:08] a close/break above equal highs is called "probably a false BOS" - so BOS validity depends on whether liquidity sits there, contradicting the pure rule.

### Internal vs external structure (swing vs substructure), structure layers
- Says: every trend contains smaller trends on lower TFs (or just smaller waves on same TF). External/swing = bigger-picture legs; internal/sub = smaller trends inside corrective legs. "You should never look to take the smaller trend movements past the lows of the larger trend" (fg [66:47]). Internal break counter-trend only justifies a short-term trade toward a point of interest within the larger leg, exit early (fg [79:51]-[85:52]). Confirmation = lower-TF trend re-agrees with HTF (fg [65:46], [73:50]).
- Proposed: two pivot sets: external N_ext (or HTF), internal N_int (or LTF). Counter-trend (substructure) trade allowed only if internal BOS occurs AND POI/target lies inside external leg AND POI not yet touched. Trend-agreement trigger = LTF BOS in direction of HTF bias after a correction began.
- Ambiguity: "internal" is relative; he alternates between "drop a time frame" and "same TF smaller waves". No rule for number of layers.

### Imbalance / FVG / inefficiency

Inefficiency measured from the zone (incl. wick) to current price (fg [139:22]-[140:22]) - i.e. in fg the "imbalance" is the whole unretested range between zone and current price, not just the 3-candle gap. ## Complete trade setups / entry models

Common skeleton: HTF direction by swing structure -> POI (S/D zone with imbalance, first tap) -> liquidity filter -> optional LTF confirmation -> entry -> stop beyond zone/swing -> target at prior swing/unfilled zone. RR is STRUCTURAL (whatever distance to target), not fixed; he prefers deep-entry zones to get 3-7R.

1. **Equal-high sweep + supply (Jh, also 7t)**: Bearish trend (several LL/LH, bearish impulses). Find EQH / resistance inside the consolidation of the trend. Wait for price to push above EQH. Zone = supply (last candle before bearish impulse that broke structure; example candle with big wick and open=close). Entry: sell limit at the supply. Stop: just above the sweep high (or above zone if near). Target: next swing low or demand zone (consolidation before the up move). TF: "any" (example chart not named; looks like intraday forex), session none. Mirror for buys with EQL + demand. Claim: 4.69R on the example.
2. **Liquidity + demand (7t)**: Find equal lows/support with 3 taps (GBPUSD 30m); identify demand under/at that area in direction of larger trend; limit buy at demand; stop below; target prior high. Same as 1 but without the structural-trend requirement spelled out beyond "direction of larger trend".
3. **Trend line reverse-engineer (Vv)**: Uptrend by HH/HL; draw ascending TL only to locate retail liquidity; find demand below TL; buy limit at demand; stop below the low (last higher low); target previous high. "Struct change = trade wrong": if higher low breaks, exit/invalid. Claim 3-4R+.
4. **Inducement / true-zone (AI)**: Uptrend (HH/HL, 1H). Candidate demand zones A (top), B (middle), C (extreme/low). Reject A because unfilled imbalance below it; reject C because it didn't by itself break structure (price had to rebuild); true = B. Wait for price to drop through A region into/just past B (wick sweep of low), go to 10/15m, wait for LTF BOS (close above LTF high) and mark LTF demand (last candle before impulse); buy limit there; stop below the low; target previous highs. Notes it called in real time (screenshot to team) but breakdown is hindsight.
5. **Imbalance-fill-then-zone (Gt)**: Open imbalance created by large candle away from supply/demand. Do not trade the gap itself. Wait for full fill so that price taps the zone (gold: demand at ~2150-2165 formed by doji-style candle before large push). Entry: limit/zone. Stop: below previous low (swing) rather than zone edge. Target: higher highs/open imbalances; ATH case uses highs. Mirror for shorts. Reasons given: avoids FVG subjectivity, better RR, FVGs mostly fully filled anyway; admits fewer trades.
6. **Basic pro-trend S/D (fg [132:19]-[135:20]; 7O)**: Bull structure (consecutive BOS); strong candles (no upper wicks, large bodies) confirm momentum; zone = last candle before the impulse; unfilled imbalance between zone and price; buy limit at zone; stop under zone; target new highs (stated "random target"). Example described as "quite lucky run".
7. **Inefficient supply with premium magnet (fg [139:22]-[145:25])**: Large bearish impulse leaves open range up to supply; wait for fill, sell at supply (limit with stop above, or confirmation entry); stop into the efficient area above (tight), target low or extended old imbalance. Notes the sell limit can fail ("this would have lost").
8. **Efficient-range reversal (fg [149:26]-[156:31])**: If price breaks through the last inefficiency into an efficient range, supply has failed -> flip.
