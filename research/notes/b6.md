## Concept definitions (merged)

### Swing high/low and structure
- Says: Highs/lows are only valid if confirmed by a candle body close beyond the prior one; wicks "do not validate highs or lows" (MS2 [19:10],[34:20]). Structure viewed as "waves", ignoring small chop (MS2 [21:10]; MS1 [01:01]).
- Objective: No pivot parameter is ever given. Implement as a zigzag on closes: pivot high = highest high of leg between two body-close breaks; require leg amplitude >= k*ATR(14) (k ~1.5-3) or fractal(N=3-5) on the structural TF. Break = candle CLOSE beyond the pivot's high/low price (close > pivot high), not high > pivot high. Trend = last confirmed BOS direction.
- Ambiguity: which pivot is "notable"/"significant" is eyeballed. He never defines "lowest point of the leg" (pivot is the extreme wick even though closes validate). Zigzag amplitude threshold is entirely discretionary. Sequence "HH then HL" is only labelled after the fact.

### BOS / "CHoCH" / structure shift
- Says: BOS = break into a new high or low; he does not use CHoCH/MSS (MS2 [09:05] "I don't use CHoCH, MSS"). In SWP he nonetheless uses "market structure shift" for HH/HL -> LL ("standard confirmation"/"standard structure shift") and "break of structure" for continuation.
- Objective: BOS_up = close > last confirmed swing high; BOS_down = close < last confirmed swing low. Continuation BOS = same direction as trend; Shift ("standard confirmation") = BOS opposite to trend, usually on a LTF.
- Ambiguity: He uses BOS and "structure shift" interchangeably and says in SWP that a shift may be a sweep (see below), so the label alone has no predictive content. Trend-flip rule differs: MS2 says a LL close below the HL flips trend; TL says only breaking the notable LH flips it.

### Internal vs external structure; impulse vs correction; swing vs substructure
- Says: Impulse = move that breaks a prior high/low; correction = move that stays inside the impulse range. Everything inside = internal; ignore it (MS2 [25:13]). MS1: swing structure = impulses, substructure = corrections, tradable on LTF.
- Objective: Maintain external range [last confirmed swing low, last confirmed swing high]. Any BOS inside the range that does not close outside it is internal. Flag trend flip only when a body close exits the external range on the opposite side. Substructure = same algorithm on a TF 3-4x lower, restricted to windows when HTF is in a correction leg.
- Ambiguity: "a long time of sideways chop is still one correction", but a correction "for a year" is not specified; a correction that makes a LL is internal until the close breaks, so external range is only known after the fact. He does admit sub-structure shifts count when taken with BE/trailing (MS1) - contradicts "ignore internal structure" in MS2. His rule is "ignore internal, except as the LTF confirmation".

### Consolidation / no-trade
- Says: if HH then LL then HH ("a mess") stay out (MS2 [07:03],[36:21]).
- Objective: no trade if the last two external structure legs have conflicting direction (HH followed by LL close without HL/LH sequencing), or ADX/range-compression filter. No threshold given.
- Ambiguity: fully discretionary.

### Supply & demand zone / order block
- Says: zone = "last candle before the impulse", any colour, indecision-style, high to low incl. wicks (SD1 [06:02]-[08:02]; SD2 [03:00]). Extend to a following candle's wick if it exceeds. Not the "last opposite-colour candle" and "back test them if you want".
- Objective: find impulse candle(s): body >= m*ATR(14) (m ~1.5) with close in top/bottom 20% of range, or an N-candle run whose net move >= X*ATR; zone candle = candle immediately before the first impulse candle. Zone = [low, high] of that candle (optionally union with the next candle's wick). Require the impulse to also close beyond a prior swing (BOS) for "strong" zone (IND2, TL).
- Ambiguity: "impulse" is visual. When several small indecision candles precede, which one? He says "last", but picks the doji look in practice (eg SD1 [13:09]); body size of zone candle is never bounded. Zone TF for refinement is a judgment call.

### Imbalance (FVG-like) and efficient vs inefficient
- Says: Imbalance = "open price range ... not filled yet with wicks"; defined as wick-to-wick gap: candle N-1 wick and candle N+1 wick do not overlap around the impulse candle (SD1 [14:09]-[17:11]). Zone is valid only if an imbalance exists into it. Inefficient = open imbalance; efficient = every zone has been retested by wicks (LIQ [08:04]; WYK [16:16]).
- Objective: 3-candle FVG: bullish gap if low[i+1] > high[i-1], bearish if high[i+1] < low[i-1], bounded by those prices. Filled when any later bar's wick enters it (full fill) - he treats wick touch as filled. Zone has "imbalance into it" if an unfilled FVG exists in the impulse leg leaving the zone (between zone and the leg's extreme) / or zone not yet touched since creation. Efficient range = sequence of zones each touched after creation.
- Ambiguity: partial vs full fill unspecified ("this wick touched it" counts). Whether imbalance must be adjacent to the zone candle or anywhere in the leg is inconsistent. Retest by wick vs close: SD1 treats wicks as removing imbalance; WYK uses closures into efficient range for stops. Applied on any TF.

### First-tap / zone mitigation
- Says: zone only good once; "fifth tap breaks" (SD1 [17:11]-[19:11]); but in SD1 [35:19] repeated taps with a close above the zone show strength; LIQ says each tap of a high/low weakens that level.
- Objective: zone.touches counter; trade only when touches == 0 prior to the entry bar; mark zone invalid after a close beyond far edge.
- Ambiguity: wick poke vs close; whether a wick fill kills the zone (SD2 [13:06] says yes). Does it hold for liquidity levels? contradicts (levels weaker with each tap, first tap of zones best).

### Extreme zone and zone selection
- Says: extreme zone = furthest imbalanced zone from price within the current leg; use limit orders there (SD1 [20:11]-[24:13]). IND2 adds: the extreme is NOT always best - a zone that did not produce a BOS (only a correction) is weaker than the lower/later zone that did.
- Objective: among valid zones in the current impulse leg pick the farthest from price, then drop it if (a) zone is on wrong side of 50% of leg, (b) equal highs/lows within X ATR beyond it, (c) it did not coincide with a BOS-producing impulse. Rank-score instead of boolean.
- Ambiguity: SD1 says "extreme = best" while IND2/SD2/IND1 say sometimes the extreme is the inducement-free choice and sometimes "lower zone" with imbalance wins. Direct contradiction between SD1 (always the extreme) and IND2 (extreme can be bad). Needs score.

### Liquidity: EQH/EQL, range liquidity, PDH/PDL, session highs/lows
- Says: Liquidity = resting orders (stops, breakout orders) above highs/below lows; EQH/EQL = two (or three) price points reacting at "the same exact level" (IND2 [03:01]); range liquidity = highs/lows of confined range (SWP); PDH/PDL and Asia range high/low quite often swept (LIQ [00:00]; SES [06:04]).
- Objective: EQH = >=2 swing highs within tol = 0.1-0.25 ATR (or <=2-3 pips on FX majors 15m) with no higher high between them. Range liquidity = highest high/lowest low of a consolidation box (>=3 bars touching both sides). PDH/PDL = prior day (broker 17:00 NY) high/low. Asia range = 00:00-07:00 UK high/low.
- Ambiguity: tolerance never given; "almost equal highs" accepted. Liquidity on TF where it was seen vs TF below ("drop to 15m to see it", IND2 [05:01]) - so liquidity is hindsight-flexible (look at whichever TF shows equal levels).

### Liquidity sweep vs break (and "sweep = failed structure shift")
- Says: Sweep = wick beyond level (rejection) that closes back; break/validated = body closure (MS2 [31:17]-[33:19]; CLO). Added in SWP: even a closed BOS can be a "sweep" if the structure shifts out of no-man's land and a deeper imbalanced demand/supply exists.
- Objective: sweep_high: high > level and close <= level on same bar (or within n=1-3 bars). Break: close > level. "Sweep-confirmed reversal": sweep then LTF BOS opposite within n bars, enter pullback (see setups).
- Ambiguity: SWP's second kind (closed breaks reclassified as sweeps when price later reverses at a demand zone) is hindsight: "while demand is intact it is a sweep, if it fails, it's a real break". Cannot be coded without a rule on how many closes beyond are allowed before flipping. No bar count.

### Inducement
- Says: a near zone that traps traders prior to a deeper "true zone". Detect by (a) equal highs/lows adjacent to it, (b) open imbalance between it and a farther zone, (c) premium/discount, (d) weaker zone strength (IND2 [20:00s]; IND1; SD2 [18:10]-[22:11]). Also trendline liquidity (TL). Not the SMC definition (first minor pullback high/low before an OB).
- Objective: for candidate zone Z in a leg: inducement if exists deeper valid zone D (unfilled, imbalance-bearing, created the BOS, in discount/premium correct) AND (EQ level within tol on the Z side OR unfilled FVG between Z and D). Trade D only. Score-based.
- Ambiguity: highest ambiguity. He picks D as "the zone that created the BOS" in some examples and as "the lowest" in others. Rules (3 factors) are evaluated visually and the zone he then picks always happens to be the one the market reacted from in shown examples.

### Premium/discount, Fibonacci, golden zone
- Says: Fib from swing to swing (left to right, in impulse direction); 50% splits premium/discount; buy only in discount, sell only in premium (FIB [02:01]; IND2 [08:03]). "Golden zone" = 0.71-0.88 retracement, "from my extensive testing", entries only there; favours 0.78 near demand; 0.71 as limit (FIB [07:02]-[12:07]).
- Objective: leg = [last confirmed swing low, swing high] (for longs). retr = (high - price)/(high - low). Premium/discount: retr > 0.5 is discount for buys. Entry limit at 0.71/0.786/0.88 or zone-in-band. Stop beyond swing low (or zone far edge).
- Ambiguity: which swing pair (can use internal structure legs "for confirmation entries") is unspecified; zone vs Fib interplay chosen after the fact. IND2 uses 50% for zone filtering by "does zone overlap discount" while FIB uses 0.71-0.88 for entry - two different filters. Admits he misses trades if price doesn't retrace there.

### Candle momentum / multi-candle momentum / candle closure
- Says: close at extreme with no opposing wick = strong (CAN [05:04]-[07:06]); long wick against the move = reversal; engulfing candle = structure shift; range of candles: slow+choppy+many candles = weak, few big fast candles that engulf prior range = strong (CAN [24:18]-[28:21]). Always wait for close; use setup-TF close (CLO [14:10]).
- Objective: strength = (close-open)/(high-low) >= 0.8 and range >= 1.5*ATR; wick ratio = opposing wick / range <= 0.1. Multi-candle: net displacement/ATR over n bars vs previous n bars; engulf = body(i) covers body(i-1) (he specifies bodies, not full ranges) or close beyond the last k swing highs. Evaluate only at bar close.
- Ambiguity: "huge" and "tiny wick" undefined, comparison to previous legs by eye. Engulfing here has a mixture of body-engulf (definition) and range-engulfing "of 7 candles" (usage).

### Wyckoff / accumulation / distribution / spring

Required close back above range high is much stricter than his own example (he accepted "a break over structural high" in WYK [14:14] then chose full range). Distribution example he declined because it had wicks only - rule is "no closes, no trade".

- Ambiguity: line selection arbitrary; he contradicts MS2 (trendlines "objectively incorrect") with TL (useful as inducement marker).

## Complete trade setups / entry models

**S1. Trend-aligned imbalanced zone limit (core model; SD1, SD2, IND1)**
- Bias: HTF (4H typical; also weekly/daily narratives) trend from body-close HH/HL (buy) or LL/LH (sell). No trade in consolidation.
- POI: last candle before impulse (high to low, wick-extended), unretested, with open imbalance into it, in discount for buys / premium for sells, pick the "extreme" (furthest in leg) or the BOS-creating zone; reject zones with EQH/EQL beyond.
- Trigger: none (limit order at the zone). Alternative "confirmation" variant below.
- Stop: just beyond zone, or beyond next structural swing (conservative).
- Target: nearest opposing imbalanced zone / previous swing / range end; RR structural (examples 4.5-7R; "1:5" quoted for one). Not fixed RR.
- Mgmt: none specified in the main model beyond full-target or stop; refine entry via 1-step-lower TF zone to halve stop.
- Pairs shown: GBPUSD 4H (SD2), EURUSD weekly (SD1); indices of other pairs unspecified. Claims: "even at 50% win rate the RR makes big profit".

**S2. Standard-confirmation entry (SD1, IND1, IND2, SWP, TL, CAN)**
- Bias: HTF trend and HTF zone touched (or price at inducement-cleared true zone).
- Trigger: on LTF, a body-close break of the latest LTF swing opposite to the prior small trend (HH/HL -> LL for sell) - "lower time frame structural shift" - plus engulfing candle (CAN) optionally.
- Entry: limit/market at the LTF supply/demand zone (last candle before the LTF impulse) formed in that shift; stop above the LTF high / zone; target previous HTF swing or opposing zone.
- RR is lower than S1, probability said to be higher. Confirmation may never come ("then no trade").

**S3. Inducement avoidance / true-zone entry (IND1, IND2, SD2)**
- As S1 with elimination: discard near zones with (a) EQ liquidity at/behind them, (b) open FVG leading to a farther zone, (c) wrong side of 50%, (d) zone did not create BOS. Trade surviving zone via limit (or confirmation variant: wait for inducement taken + true-zone tap + LTF BOS, enter LTF supply). Stop beyond zone; target lows/highs.

**S4. Sweep-vs-reversal fade (SWP)**
- Context: HTF uptrend (say), price at no significant demand, imbalanced demand lies below. A closed break of the swing low is treated as a sweep: expect the move into the lower demand then continuation up (buy at demand after LTF reaction).
- Range-liquidity short: price sweeps range high, then LTF HH/HL -> LL shift ("standard confirmation"), sell pullback at imbalanced supply, stop above, target range low; repeat each new sweep until demand reached.
- USDJPY real-time example; outcome of the HTF idea not shown ("we never got to see the outcome").

Look for sweep of one side into/around London open, fill of a gap/supply, LTF reversal (BOS) and supply entry just before London open; stop above sweep high; target imbalanced demand / Asia opposite extreme; exit by London close. NY: trade structural reversal vs London at NY open. Pair: EURUSD 15m (+ majors); exotics (AUDJPY) for Asia.

**S7. Simplified Wyckoff (WYK)**

## Quant red flags
- Hindsight everywhere: pivot validity, "extreme zone", "efficient/inefficient", "first tap", "true zone vs inducement", "sweep vs reversal" are classified with the full leg visible. SWP states bias flips only "with the benefit of hindsight". Any rule built at bar t must use only information through t.
- Zone and swing detection are visual: no ATR/pip thresholds for impulse, indecision, equal highs, or swing size. Almost every filter (premium/discount, imbalance, liquidity, strength) is combined by judgment; the filter set is under-determined and examples were selected where the answer worked. Four to six filters evaluated in the same trade produce very few signals; selection of which one is 'decisive' differs by video.
- Filter contradiction: SD1 says take the extreme zone; IND2/IND1 say the extreme can be an inducement trap. SD1: price taps weaken zones; LIQ: price taps weaken levels, so the same tap count has opposite sign for zones vs levels. MS2: trendlines "objectively incorrect"; TL: trendline fake-outs are "the best trades". MS2 forbids internal structure trading; MS1 teaches it as substructure. CHoCH rejected as term but the same event is "standard confirmation".
- Close-vs-wick rules can't be both: wick break = rejection (reversal signal) yet a closed break can also be a "sweep" (SWP). Needs a time/bar-count rule.
- Survivorship: all charts are winners; losing zone examples (e.g. SD1's "fifth tap" etc.) are used to explain rules afterwards. Quoted "60% of days" and 53% win rate / 5.85 avg RR are unaudited, small samples; 53% win with 5.85 RR on 1% risk is inconsistent with typical limit-order retests at 3-4R results he shows (LIQ 3.38R, WYK ~5R) and 16% over 3 months at 1% risk per trade implies roughly 3 trades/month or smaller risk - check. "Risk of ruin <0.01%" derived from the same unaudited stats.
- Entry mechanics unrealistic for backtest: limit at the zone edge assumes fill, not accounting for wick-through-without-fill, spread, slippage; 3.4 pip stops on 2m with 1:30 leverage; fills on "third tap" etc. Tight refined-zone stops (half-size) double sizing without regard to spread.
- Session model: time-zone/DST not specified; "Asia sweep" defined on a range he draws by an indicator (FXN Asian session range); NY reversal vs continuation chosen after the fact.
- Multi-timeframe closures need consistent resampling (open at TF boundary, broker/NY-17:00 day) or the "setup TF close" logic will leak LTF info.
- Fibonacci golden zone 0.71-0.88 is "from my testing" but no numbers; swing pair choice is subjective. Easily overfit.
- Percent returns quoted (5.47%, 6.85%, 7.05%, 14%) are chart-hindsight targets with 1% risk and are not expected values.
